No deadline to sort by — which is not the same as no deadline

Foundations publish dates far less reliably than agencies do. Some review on a rolling basis and genuinely have no deadline; some post one only when a cycle opens; and some serve a page no parser can read, because the text arrives by JavaScript. These three cases look identical in a blank date column, so each row says which one it is. Where it says unreadable, open the page yourself — there may well be a date on it.

Early-career routes — for the lab, not for the PI

8 programs the PI is ineligible to hold

A large share of the best-known foundation awards are restricted to faculty in their first years — Sloan, Searle, Packard, Klingenstein, Pew, Rita Allen, Beckman. For a senior PI these are not weak fits, they are ineligible. They stay on this page because the lab still uses them: they are what a departing postdoc applies for on taking a first faculty job, and knowing the deadline a year ahead is what makes that possible. Same reason the NIH page keeps its F32 and K99 rows.

Before committing to any of these

Foundation constraints with no federal equivalent

Limited submission means an internal deadline

Where a program is marked limited, the institution may forward only a capped number of applicants — sometimes one. The operative deadline is therefore the internal campus deadline, months earlier, and the first competition is against colleagues, not against the field. Missing it means the foundation deadline is irrelevant. Check the sponsored-projects limited-submission calendar before anything else.

Nomination means you cannot apply

Where a program is marked nomination, only a nominating official may put a name forward, and for several the foundation invites a fixed list of institutions. A strong application written without a nomination is not a long shot — it will not be read.

Fit is judged by a program officer, not a panel

Foundations rarely run standing study sections. A program officer or a small board decides, often on a written scope that is narrower than the public page suggests. A short exploratory email is normal here and costs nothing; at NIH it would be unusual.

Indirect-cost rates are usually capped

Most private foundations cap indirect costs well below a federally negotiated rate — 10–15 per cent is common and some pay none. That is an institutional matter requiring approval, not a detail to settle at submission. Budget the direct costs accordingly.

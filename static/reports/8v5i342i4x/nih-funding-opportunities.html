What the landscape itself says

Four findings that change how the lab should target

Thin field

412 active NOFOs across all 27 institutes and offices. NCI alone holds 56 of them. Targeted basic-neuroscience solicitations are genuinely scarce right now.

NEI is nearly absent

NEI is primary institute on exactly two active NOFOs — a secondary-data-analysis R21 and a clinical study-planning R34.

There is currently no NEI-specific basic vision research announcement. That work has to go through the parent R01 or through BRAIN Initiative RFAs.

NOSIs have wound down

The Notice of Special Interest route has largely closed. Across every Guide notice since December 2023, the most recent NOSI dates to May 2025, and essentially all recent NOSI-related notices are expirations or participations rather than new topic solicitations.

If the lab previously relied on finding a relevant NOSI and applying to the parent R01, targeting now means matching a specific RFA or PAR — or going in cold on the parent.

A lost R21 route

NINDS let PA-25-150 expire — the joint NINDS/NIMH Exploratory Neuroscience Research Grant, per NOT-NS-26-007 in March 2026.

That was a common basic-neuroscience R21 route. The generic parent R21 (PA-25-304) is now the replacement.

If the goal is the autumn 2026 cycle

Highest-yield combination, in the order I would write them

  1. RFA-NS-27-001 — U24, 6 October. Dissemination of the existing toolkit. Best fit-to-effort ratio in the whole scan: budgets uncapped, ten to twenty awards available.
  2. RFA-DA-27-004 — R01, 6 October. Encoding-model methodology framed as BRAIN theories, models and methods.
  3. PA-25-301 — Parent R01, 5 October. The core science aims, NEI or NIMH assignment.
  4. RFA-RM-27-001 — DP1, 9 September. Only if there is an idea worth the single-PI, high-risk framing, and only if it can be written in six weeks.
  5. RFA-OD-24-011 — R50, 4 December. Last cycle, cheap to write, funds a maintainer.

Items one and two share the 6 October deadline and draw on overlapping material, but they are scientifically distinct — dissemination of existing resources versus development of new methods — so they are not "highly overlapping" under NIH policy.

Standing Broad Agency Announcements

Open continuously — the real route for an unsolicited idea

These have a close date a year or more out, which is not a deadline in any useful sense. A BAA is a standing invitation covering a whole agency's research interests: you send a white paper first, it is reviewed as it arrives, and a program officer decides whether to invite a full proposal. Talk to the program officer before writing anything. They are invisible to topic keywords because they are written to cover everything.

Before committing to any of these

DoD-specific constraints with no NIH or NSF equivalent

Contracts, not grants

Many DoD BAAs are issued under FAR Part 35 and result in a contract rather than an assistance award. Deliverables, reporting and IP terms differ, and the institutional route through the sponsored projects office is not the same one an R01 takes.

Two-stage submission

The white paper is the real gate. Full proposals are usually by invitation, so an uninvited full proposal is wasted effort.

Program officers matter

DoD basic research is program-officer driven to a degree NIH study sections are not. At DARPA especially, the program manager defines the program and turns over every few years; an idea that does not match a current PM's program will not be funded no matter how good it is.

Restrictions to check early

Some announcements carry citizenship or security requirements, export-control (ITAR/EAR) terms, or publication review clauses. These are a matter of institutional policy as much as personal preference — check them before investing in a white paper, not after.
